## The 3-step review

Every thesis chapter, course module, or research paper goes through:

### Step 1: Self-review (author)

| Check | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Argument | Is the claim clear and supported? |
| Evidence | Are all claims cited? |
| Counter-arguments | Have I addressed opposing views? |
| Clarity | Would a smart non-expert understand? |
| Length | Is it tight (no filler)? |

**Output**: Self-review checklist completed.

### Step 2: Peer review (1 person + 1 agent)

| Reviewer | Focus |
|----------|-------|
| **Kiki** (or Ivan's co-author) | Technical accuracy, methodology rigor |
| **Agent** (`research-tracker` or `kiki-coach`) | Citation coverage, structure, clarity |

**Output**: Comments document (`~/peer-reviews/{topic}-{date}.md`).

### Step 3: Final review (Ivan)

- Re-reads the work after revisions
- Approves for submission / publication / shipping
- Triggers next-step (journal submission, course release, etc.)

---

## Acceptance criteria per step

| Step | Must pass before next |
|------|----------------------|
| 1 | Self-review checklist complete |
| 2 | All reviewer comments addressed (or noted as "won't fix" with reason) |
| 3 | Final approval documented |

---

## Escalation

If reviewers disagree on substance:
- **Step 2 disagreement**: bring to Ivan for adjudication
- **Step 3 disagreement**: defer publication until resolved
- **Style/substance split**: style is non-blocking; substance is blocking

## When this isn't needed

- Internal PROMPT.md files (technical specs, not research)
- Sales playbooks (operational, not research)
- Marketing copy (not research)
- One-off debugging notes (operational)

The 3-step process applies only to research outputs that will be **published or shipped externally** (thesis, course, paper, blog post).
